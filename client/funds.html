If the same company profile remains missing, examine the route by which it should become visible

Virtus works with funds that can describe a specific sourcing problem and are prepared to distinguish broader discovery from more activity inside the same network.

A fund can spend considerable time sourcing while repeatedly encountering the same part of the market. More introductions do not necessarily broaden the sample if those introductions travel through connected relationships, similar institutions, and founders already practiced at attracting venture attention. The result can be a busy pipeline that leaves a stated area of interest persistently underrepresented.

Virtus approaches that condition as a discovery problem that may also contain a recognition problem. The operator may sit outside the fund's ordinary network. The company may emerge from research, public service, defense, health, engineering, or a workforce path that produces evidence in an unfamiliar form. The opportunity may be present while lacking the language, affiliation, or advocates that usually move a company into view. A founder may describe customer work as deployment rather than traction, or may speak about reliability before market size because reliability is what the buyer first requires.

A usable mandateName the evidence, constraints, and risks the partnership can actually hold.

A category is not yet a search

“Technical founders” or “regulated industries” can express an interest, but neither phrase tells us enough to search responsibly. We need to understand the stage at which the partnership can act, the evidence it considers meaningful, the geography it can support, the customer and regulatory risks it understands, and the conditions that have caused apparently relevant companies to fall outside the mandate in the past.

This specificity narrows activity while improving relevance. It prevents us from sending companies connected only by a broad label, and it gives operators a clearer basis for deciding whether to accept a conversation. It can also expose a gap between the partnership's language and its actual investment behavior. That is useful information. A mandate becomes more credible when it can survive contact with a company that fits the underlying need but does not resemble the firm's existing network.

The objective is not proprietary volume. It is a better reason for the right conversation to occur.

How we workInstitutional relationships are maintained before and after a fund presents a search.

Discovery through institutions with an earlier view

These institutions see companies and technical people in the course of responsibilities that are not primarily venture sourcing. Their perspective can therefore contain context that is absent from a conventional inbound funnel.

We do not treat these relationships as lists. A search has to respect the purpose of the institution and the position of the operator. When a possible connection emerges, our role is to determine whether the reason for an introduction can be stated plainly, whether both parties have enough context to agree to it, and whether the match deserves time before any implication of investment interest is created.

The basis for working together today is the specificity of the search, the care of the method, and the quality of the context around an introduction. If your partnership has been unable to find a profile it can define clearly, send us the mandate and the history of the search.
