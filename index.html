Good companies and patient capital get built three miles apart and never meet.

Virtus Ventures works the seam between them. We find the operators the market has not priced yet, and we put them in front of the people who should have found them first.

Read the thesis Bring us a company

The failure is not a shortage of money or a shortage of talent.

There is more uncommitted venture capital sitting idle right now than the market knows what to do with, and there are more technically serious founders in the Baltimore and Washington corridor than any single fund has the hours to meet. Both of those facts are true at the same time, in the same region, and they stay true year after year because nothing in between them is doing its job.

What is scarce is not capital and it is not talent. What is scarce is attention that has been spent well, and the willingness to look somewhere that does not already have a line out the door.

Most sourcing is a popularity contest dressed up as diligence.

Deal flow arrives sorted by visibility, not by quality.

An investor sees the founders who know how to be seen. Demo days, accelerator cohorts, warm introductions passed between funds that already share a portfolio, and the same handful of names circulating through the same rooms. That process is efficient at surfacing people who are good at being surfaced, and it is close to useless at surfacing an engineer who has been quietly building something durable for four years without a single tweet about it.

The founders who are hardest to find are frequently the ones worth finding, and the entire discovery machine is pointed away from them.

So we do the part nobody is paid to do.

We go and look. We work through the state economic development offices, the university labs, the veteran operator networks, and the regional programs that see companies eighteen months before a fund ever will, and we build real relationships with the people running them. When a fund tells us the profile it cannot find, that groundwork is what lets us answer instead of guess.

None of this scales the way software scales, and it is not supposed to. We work with a small number of companies at a time because the work is judgment, and judgment does not survive being run at volume.
